## Conventions

- 1 Blender unit = 1 stud. Blender (X, Y, Z) maps to Roblox (X, Z, -Y), so Blender +Y (the working end) becomes the Roblox LookVector.
- Handheld tools have their origin at the hand grip. The Rotary Sweeper and Ride-on have theirs at ground level under the body. Moving parts have theirs on their axis.
- Studio puts a MeshPart's CFrame at its bounding-box centre. Use `originFromBoxCentre` in `manifest.json` to set Tool Grips and Motor6D/hinge C0s.
- Look: smoothed low poly (owner, 2026-11-26). Every hard piece has a small one-segment bevel, round parts have more sides, and shading is smooth with edges sharper than 50 deg kept crisp. Weighted normals are baked into the mesh so big faces stay evenly lit. One SmoothPlastic MeshPart per mesh, colour only from the shared texture. No Roblox material textures and no neon.
- Tickets: the regular atlas leaves the border band, the notch rim, the paper edge and the perforation gaps **transparent**, so `MeshPart.Color` shows through. One mesh and one texture make every colour variant. The 20 test tints are `TINTS` in `build_tickets.py`. The gold ticket is fully opaque and has its own outline with rounded corners and rounded notches.
- Ticket print (all in the atlas, nothing to add in Studio):
  - Front: sunburst behind the stars, "LUCKY TICKET" / "GOLDEN TICKET" caption, "TICKET No." label, number, and a silver scratch panel marked "SCRATCH".
  - Back: "GOOD LUCK!" / "WINNER" caption, number, barcode.
  - Gold only: sparkles and a printed frame.
  - The regular ticket is at 36 of its 40-tri cap, so its corners stay square.

- Sizes arrived exactly 1:1.
- The importer turns the meshes 180 deg about Y.
  - In a MeshPart's own frame, Blender (x, y, z) maps to (-x, z, y), so the working end points to local +Z.
  - Real local offset = (-x, y, -z) of `manifest.json` `originFromBoxCentre`.
  - The templates already correct for this.
- Templates: `ServerStorage.AssetKit`.
  - `Tools.<ToolCatalog Id>`: Model with PrimaryPart `Body`. The pivot is on the grip (handhelds) or the ground (vehicles), and its LookVector is the working end.
  - Attachments `Grip` / `HandleGrip` / `Seat`. Moving parts have a `PivotOffset` on their axis and an `Axis` attachment whose X axis is the spin/hinge axis.
  - `Tickets.Regular`, `Tickets.Golden`, `Tickets.Tints` (20 Color3Values).
  - `Imported/` holds the untouched package copies.
- All template parts: anchored, SmoothPlastic, box collision. Handhelds and tickets `CanCollide = false`; vehicle bodies `CanCollide = true`.
- **Required:** `RenderFidelity = Precise` on every template part. With Automatic, Roblox's distance LOD drops whole disconnected pieces (wheels, fenders, bodies).
- **Required for tinting:** a plain `MeshPart.TextureID` draws see-through pixels BLACK. Regular tickets use a `SurfaceAppearance` (ColorMap = the ticket atlas, `AlphaMode = Overlay`) with TextureID cleared, so `MeshPart.Color` fills the band. Gold keeps TextureID because it is opaque and sharper that way.

## Bag frame

Origin = centre of an R6 torso's **back face**. In Roblox: `bag:PivotTo(torso.CFrame * CFrame.new(0, 0, 0.5))`. The template pivot's LookVector is the wearer's facing direction. Attachment `Body.BodyBack` sits on the pivot (for a weld or RigidConstraint). All bag parts are Massless, CanCollide/CanTouch false. The Wagon also has `Body.HandGrip` at the R6 right hand (torso-relative (1.5, -1.1, -0.45)); it is welded at the back like the others, so the arm animation does not swing it.
